# Post-Pandemic-Causes-of-School-Attendance-Problems

### By: 
Ashley Goldstein, Karina R. Carrillo-Juarez, Skylar Brown, Randolph Dupont, Michael Fensken, Christopher A. Kearney  
University of Nevada, Las Vegas - Department of Psychology

---

## Summary  
This project explores how the underlying reasons for school refusal behavior may have shifted following the COVID-19 pandemic. Using data collected from UNLV's School Refusal Clinic, we compared pre-pandemic and post-pandemic cases to better understand patterns in school attendance problems (SAPs).

## Research Question  
- How have the causes underlying school refusal behavior changed following the COVID-19 pandemic?  
- We hypothesized that each of the four known subscales would increase significantly from the pre- to post-pandemic group, from both child and parent perspectives.

## Methodology  
**Participants:**  
- Pre-pandemic group: N = 59 children referred to UNLV’s School Refusal Clinic  
- Post-pandemic group: N = 32 children referred post-pandemic  
- Total sample size: 91 participants (ages 5-17)

**Measures:**  
- School Refusal Assessment Scale-Revised (SRAS-R) Parent & Child Reports  
  Subscales measured:  
  - Avoidance of Negative Affectivity (ANA)  
  - Escape from Social/Evaluative situations (ESE)  
  - Pursuit of Tangible Rewards outside school (PTR)  
  - Attention-Seeking Behavior (AGB)

**Analysis:**  
- Two independent samples t-tests to compare pre- and post-pandemic groups  
- *Tools used: SPSS, Excel*

## Results  
- No significant differences were found on the child-reported SRAS-C subscales.  
- A significant difference was observed in the parent-reported ANA subscale:  
  *t(87) = .825, p = .016*  
- Parents reported lower severity of avoidance of negative affect in post-pandemic cases.

## Key Takeaways  
- Despite increases in school attendance problems globally, underlying causes of school refusal behavior remained fairly consistent before and after the pandemic.  
- Parent perceptions shifted slightly, especially regarding emotional avoidance (ANA).  
- Findings suggest that assessing and addressing ANA may be particularly helpful in treatment.

## Limitations & Future Directions  
- Conducted with a clinical sample at a single site (UNLV), which may limit generalizability.  
- Post-pandemic group had a smaller sample size.

**Future research should:**  
- Explore larger samples  
- Investigate the specific reasons behind the observed ANA difference  
- Control for additional variables (e.g., socioeconomic status, remote learning experience)
